\chapter{Mathematical and Statistical Methods}
\label{ch:mathematics}

This chapter documents the mathematics the package implements, rather than
paraphrasing the code that implements it. Every formula below corresponds to
a specific function whose reference entry is cross-linked; no theoretical
guarantee is claimed that the repository does not establish. All methods are
deterministic: given the same inputs they produce the same outputs, with no
sampling and no learned parameters.

Throughout, $\mathcal{D} = \{d_1,\dots,d_N\}$ denotes the corpus of document
chunks produced by \cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.chunking.feedback_to_chunks},
and $q$ denotes a user question.

\section{Feature-hashing embeddings}
\label{sec:math-embeddings}

\subsection*{Objective}

Map arbitrary text to a fixed-dimensional vector without a trained model,
without network access, and without persisted vocabulary, so retrieval can be
exercised end to end in tests, demos, and CI.

\subsection*{Formulation}

Let $\phi(t)$ be the multiset of features of text $t$: the unigrams and
adjacent bigrams of its lower-cased alphanumeric tokens,

\begin{equation}
\phi(t) = \{\texttt{uni:}w_i\}_{i=1}^{n}
        \;\cup\;
        \{\texttt{bi:}w_i\_w_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n-1},
\end{equation}

where $w_1,\dots,w_n$ are the tokens matched by \texttt{[a-zA-Z0-9\_]+}.

Let $h(f)$ be the 64-bit unsigned integer obtained from the BLAKE2b digest of
feature $f$ with an 8-byte digest size, interpreted big-endian. Define the
column index and the sign

\begin{align}
c(f) &= h(f) \bmod D, \\
s(f) &= \begin{cases}
  +1 & \text{if } \left\lfloor h(f)/D \right\rfloor \bmod 2 = 0,\\
  -1 & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{align}

with $D$ the embedding dimension (default $512$, constrained to $[64, 8192]$).
The unnormalised embedding is the signed feature-count vector

\begin{equation}
\tilde{x}(t)_j = \sum_{f \in \phi(t)} s(f)\,\mathbb{1}\!\left[c(f) = j\right],
\qquad j = 0,\dots,D-1,
\end{equation}

and the returned embedding is its $L^2$ normalisation

\begin{equation}
x(t) = \frac{\tilde{x}(t)}{\max\left(\lVert \tilde{x}(t) \rVert_2,\, \epsilon\right)},
\end{equation}

where the implementation replaces a zero norm by $1$ rather than dividing by
zero, so the embedding of a text with no tokens is the zero vector.

\subsection*{Assumptions and limitations}

The signed hashing trick makes the expected inner product of two hashed vectors
an unbiased estimate of the inner product of the exact (unhashed) count
vectors, with collision noise decreasing in $D$; the sign function is what
cancels collision cross-terms in expectation. This is a property of the
construction, not a claim measured in this repository. The model carries no
semantic similarity: two paraphrases sharing no tokens receive near-orthogonal
vectors. The source states this explicitly --- it exists to keep the pipeline
runnable and testable, not to compete with neural embeddings --- and the
lexical and hybrid retrievers exist partly to compensate.

\subsection*{Complexity}

Embedding $m$ texts of average token length $\bar{n}$ costs
$\mathcal{O}(m\bar{n})$ hash evaluations and $\mathcal{O}(mD)$ space.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.embeddings.HashingEmbeddingModel}.

\section{Dense retrieval by cosine similarity}
\label{sec:math-dense}

Because both stored and query vectors are $L^2$-normalised, cosine similarity
reduces to an inner product, and the store computes it as a single
matrix--vector product:

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{score}(q, d) = x(q)^{\top} x(d)
 = \cos\!\big(x(q), x(d)\big) \in [-1, 1].
\end{equation}

Retrieval returns the top $k$ chunks by this score. The implementation selects
candidates with \texttt{np.argpartition} (average $\mathcal{O}(N)$) and sorts
only the selected $k$ (\texttt{np.argsort}, $\mathcal{O}(k \log k)$), rather
than sorting all $N$ scores. Total cost per query is
$\mathcal{O}(ND + N + k\log k)$.

\paragraph{Numerical considerations.}
Normalisation bounds every score in $[-1,1]$ regardless of document length,
which is what makes the min--max fusion in \cref{sec:math-hybrid} meaningful.
The store validates that the query vector is one-dimensional with exactly $D$
components before multiplying, converting a shape error into a clear
\texttt{ValueError} rather than a broadcasting surprise.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.vector_store.InMemoryVectorStore},
\cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.retrieval.QueryEngine}.

\section{Okapi BM25 lexical retrieval}
\label{sec:math-bm25}

\subsection*{Objective}

Reward exact term matches --- product names, integration names, error codes ---
that a hashed bag-of-features representation dilutes.

\subsection*{Formulation}

For a query with terms $t_1,\dots,t_M$ (obtained by the same tokenisation as
the index) and a document $d$ with term frequency $f(t,d)$ and length $|d|$ in
tokens, the score is

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{BM25}(q,d) = \sum_{i=1}^{M}
\operatorname{IDF}(t_i)\;
\frac{f(t_i,d)\,(k_1 + 1)}
     {f(t_i,d) + k_1\left(1 - b + b\,\dfrac{|d|}{\overline{|d|}}\right)},
\end{equation}

with $\overline{|d|}$ the mean document length over the corpus. The inverse
document frequency uses the standard smoothed form

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{IDF}(t) = \ln\!\left(1 + \frac{N - n(t) + 0.5}{n(t) + 0.5}\right),
\end{equation}

where $n(t)$ is the number of documents containing $t$. Adding $1$ inside the
logarithm keeps $\operatorname{IDF}(t) > 0$ for every term, so a term occurring
in every document contributes a small positive weight rather than a negative
one --- the well-known pathology of the unsmoothed Robertson--Sp\"arck~Jones
form.

\subsection*{Parameters and validation}

The defaults are $k_1 = 1.5$ (term-frequency saturation) and $b = 0.75$ (length
normalisation strength). The constructor enforces $k_1 \geq 0$ and
$b \in [0,1]$; $b = 0$ disables length normalisation and $b = 1$ applies it
fully. The corpus statistics $f(\cdot,\cdot)$, $|d|$, $\overline{|d|}$, and
$\operatorname{IDF}(\cdot)$ are computed once at construction, so the index is
immutable for its lifetime.

\subsection*{Ranking and stability}

Only documents with a strictly positive score are returned. Ties are broken by
ascending corpus position, making the ranking a total order and the output
reproducible across runs. Scores are rounded to six decimals before being
returned.

\subsection*{Complexity}

Index construction is $\mathcal{O}\!\left(\sum_d |d|\right)$ time and
$\mathcal{O}(V + \sum_d |d|)$ space for vocabulary $V$. A query costs
$\mathcal{O}(NM)$ in the implemented form, because every document is scored
against every query term; no posting-list inversion is used, which is
acceptable at the corpus sizes this package targets and keeps the code
dependency-free.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.lexical_search.BM25Retriever}.

\section{Hybrid fusion}
\label{sec:math-hybrid}

\subsection*{Objective}

Combine two rankings whose scores live on incomparable scales: cosine
similarity in $[-1,1]$ and unbounded non-negative BM25.

\subsection*{Formulation}

Each retriever is queried for an enlarged candidate pool of size
$k' = \max(2k, k)$. Let $R_{\text{dense}}$ and $R_{\text{lex}}$ be the returned
result lists. Scores within each list are min--max normalised,

\begin{equation}
\nu(s; S) =
\begin{cases}
  \dfrac{s - \min S}{\max S - \min S} & \text{if } \max S \neq \min S,\\[8pt]
  1 & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

so a list whose scores are all equal maps to all ones rather than to a division
by zero. With user weights $w_{\text{dense}}, w_{\text{lex}} \geq 0$ normalised
to sum to one,

\begin{equation}
\bar{w}_{\text{dense}} = \frac{w_{\text{dense}}}{w_{\text{dense}} + w_{\text{lex}}},
\qquad
\bar{w}_{\text{lex}} = \frac{w_{\text{lex}}}{w_{\text{dense}} + w_{\text{lex}}},
\end{equation}

the fused score of a chunk $c$ is the weighted sum of the contributions it
receives from the lists it appears in:

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{score}(c) =
\bar{w}_{\text{dense}}\,\nu\!\big(s_{\text{dense}}(c); S_{\text{dense}}\big)\,
  \mathbb{1}[c \in R_{\text{dense}}]
+ \bar{w}_{\text{lex}}\,\nu\!\big(s_{\text{lex}}(c); S_{\text{lex}}\big)\,
  \mathbb{1}[c \in R_{\text{lex}}].
\end{equation}

Chunks are de-duplicated by chunk identifier, so a chunk retrieved by both
retrievers accumulates both contributions and is therefore favoured over one
retrieved by a single retriever at the same normalised score.

\subsection*{Properties and limitations}

Normalising the weights makes the fused score scale-free: only the ratio
$w_{\text{dense}} : w_{\text{lex}}$ matters. The defaults are $0.6 : 0.4$. Two
consequences follow directly from the definition and are worth stating plainly:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item Because normalisation is per query and per list, fused scores are not
    comparable across queries.
  \item The top-scoring item of each list always normalises to $1$, so a list
    that contains no genuinely relevant document still contributes a
    full-weight vote for its best item. Fusion improves recall; it does not
    detect that a retriever failed.
\end{itemize}

The constructor rejects negative weights and rejects the case where both are
zero.

Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.retrieval.HybridRetriever},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.retrieval.min_max_normalize}.

\section{Deterministic judge reranking}
\label{sec:math-reranking}

\subsection*{Objective}

Reorder the enlarged candidate pool with domain-aware signals, standing in for
a cross-encoder or LLM judge while remaining local and deterministic.

\subsection*{Formulation}

Let $T(q)$ be the content tokens of the question (alphabetic-initial tokens of
at least three characters, minus a fixed stop list) and let $\sigma(c)$ be the
lower-cased concatenation of the chunk text with its \texttt{customer\_segment},
\texttt{channel}, and \texttt{rating} metadata. Define four signals in $[0,1]$:

\begin{align}
o(q,c) &= \frac{\left|\{t \in T(q) : t \subseteq \sigma(c)\}\right|}{|T(q)|}
  && \text{(lexical overlap)}, \\
r(q,c) &= \min\!\left(\frac{|\{\kappa \in K(\rho) : \kappa \subseteq \sigma(c)\}|}{3}, 1\right)
  && \text{(route-keyword hits)}, \\
g(q,c) &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\text{segment of } c \text{ appears in } q\right]
  && \text{(segment match)}, \\
\ell(q,c) &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[T(q) \cap \Theta \neq \emptyset
  \;\wedge\; \operatorname{rating}(c) \leq 2\right]
  && \text{(risk signal)},
\end{align}

where $K(\rho)$ are the keywords of the route $\rho$ selected for the question
and $\Theta = \{\text{unhappy},\allowbreak \text{complain},\allowbreak
\text{complaints},\allowbreak \text{risk},\allowbreak \text{churn},\allowbreak
\text{bad},\allowbreak \text{poor}\}$. The reranked score adds a fixed linear
combination of these signals to the retrieval score $s(c)$:

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{score}(q,c) = s(c)
  + 0.22\,o(q,c)
  + 0.18\,r(q,c)
  + 0.08\,g(q,c)
  + 0.06\,\ell(q,c),
\end{equation}

rounded to six decimals, and the top $k$ are kept.

\subsection*{Interpretation and limitations}

The coefficients are fixed constants in the source. The repository contains no
tuning procedure, no held-out selection, and no sensitivity analysis for them,
so they should be read as engineering defaults rather than as fitted
parameters. The maximum possible adjustment is $0.54$, which is large relative
to a cosine score in $[-1,1]$ and to a fused hybrid score in $[0,1]$: the
reranker can and does override the first-stage ordering. Because $s(c)$ enters
additively and unnormalised, the relative influence of the signals depends on
which retriever produced $s(c)$.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.reranking.DeterministicJudgeReranker}.

\section{Answer confidence}
\label{sec:math-confidence}

The agent reports a confidence score combining retrieval quality with citation
density. With $R$ the final result set and $C$ the citations built from it,

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{conf} =
\begin{cases}
  0 & \text{if } R = \emptyset \text{ or } C = \emptyset, \\[4pt]
  \min\!\left(
    0.75 \max\!\left(\max_{c \in R} s(c),\, 0\right)
    + 0.25 \min\!\left(\dfrac{|C|}{3}, 1\right),
    \; 1
  \right) & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

rounded to three decimals. Three or more citations saturate the citation term.
A refused answer reports confidence $0$ by construction.

This is a heuristic index, not a calibrated probability: the repository
contains no calibration data and makes no claim that $\operatorname{conf}$
estimates the probability that an answer is correct.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.agent.FeedbackInsightAgent}.

\section{Evidence overlap and hallucination risk}
\label{sec:math-hallucination}

\subsection*{Objective}

Detect answers that drift away from the retrieved evidence, deterministically
and without a second model call.

\subsection*{Formulation}

Split the answer $a$ into sentences $a_1,\dots,a_S$ on terminal punctuation.
Let $\tau(\cdot)$ extract content tokens: lower-cased tokens matching
\texttt{[a-zA-Z][a-zA-Z0-9\_-]\{2,\}}, minus a fixed stop list, minus tokens
beginning with the document-identifier prefix \texttt{fb-} so citation markers
cannot be mistaken for supported content. Let $E = \tau(\text{concatenated
retrieved chunk texts})$.

A sentence is \emph{supported} when its token overlap fraction reaches the
sentence threshold $\theta_s$ (default $0.5$):

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{supported}(a_i) =
  \mathbb{1}\!\left[
    \frac{|\tau(a_i) \cap E|}{|\tau(a_i)|} \geq \theta_s
  \right],
\qquad \tau(a_i) \neq \emptyset .
\end{equation}

Sentences with no content tokens are excluded from both numerator and
denominator. With $S'$ the number of scored sentences, the overlap score is

\begin{equation}
\Omega(a, E) = \frac{1}{S'}\sum_{i : \tau(a_i) \neq \emptyset}
  \operatorname{supported}(a_i),
\end{equation}

rounded to four decimals, and the check passes when
$\Omega \geq \theta_p$ with the pass threshold $\theta_p$ default $0.75$. Both
thresholds are validated to lie in $[0,1]$.

\subsection*{Risk aggregation}

An optional LLM-as-judge returns a label
$v \in \{\text{supported}, \text{unsupported}, \text{uncertain}\}$. The risk
level is a deterministic function of the two signals, with the judge able to
escalate but never to clear a failed lexical gate:

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{risk} =
\begin{cases}
  \text{high}   & v = \text{unsupported},\\
  \text{high}   & \Omega < \theta_p,\\
  \text{medium} & v = \text{uncertain},\\
  \text{medium} & \text{some sentence is unsupported},\\
  \text{low}    & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\end{equation}

A hallucination is recorded exactly when $\operatorname{risk} = \text{high}$.

\subsection*{Limitations}

$\Omega$ is a lexical proxy for claim support. It cannot detect a fluent
restatement that contradicts the evidence while reusing its vocabulary, and it
penalises correct paraphrase that introduces new content words. The judge
parser is deliberately conservative: an unparseable or missing verdict yields
\text{uncertain} with confidence $0$ rather than \text{supported}.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.hallucination.evidence_overlap_score},
\cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.hallucination.EvidenceOverlapHallucinationChecker}.

\section{Offline evaluation metrics}
\label{sec:math-evaluation}

Let $\hat{R}_k$ be the deduplicated list of the first $k$ retrieved document
identifiers for a case, preserving first-seen order, and let $G$ be the set of
relevant document identifiers declared for that case. Deduplication is applied
before scoring so that returning the same relevant document repeatedly cannot
inflate any metric.

\begin{align}
\operatorname{P@}k &= \frac{|\hat{R}_k \cap G|}{|\hat{R}_k|},
  \qquad \operatorname{P@}k = 0 \text{ when } \hat{R}_k = \emptyset, \\
\operatorname{R@}k &= \frac{|\hat{R}_k \cap G|}{|G|},
  \qquad \operatorname{R@}k = 0 \text{ when } G = \emptyset, \\
\operatorname{RR} &= \begin{cases}
  \dfrac{1}{\min\{i : \hat{R}_i \in G\}} & \text{if such an } i \text{ exists},\\
  0 & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases} \\
\operatorname{Hit} &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\hat{R} \cap G \neq \emptyset\right].
\end{align}

Both $\operatorname{P@}k$ and $\operatorname{R@}k$ require $k > 0$ and raise
otherwise. Note that $\operatorname{R@}k$ returning $0$ for an empty $G$ is a
deliberate convention for unanswerable cases: they contribute no recall rather
than a vacuous $1$.

Answer-side metrics, with $\mathcal{K}$ the expected keywords:

\begin{align}
\operatorname{KeywordCoverage}
  &= \frac{|\{\kappa \in \mathcal{K} : \kappa \subseteq \operatorname{lower}(a)\}|}
          {|\mathcal{K}|},
  \qquad = 1 \text{ when } \mathcal{K} = \emptyset, \\
\operatorname{Groundedness}
  &= \frac{1}{S'}\sum_{i} \mathbb{1}\!\left[
      \frac{|\tau(a_i) \cap E|}{|\tau(a_i)|} \geq 0.5\right], \\
\operatorname{RefusalCorrect}
  &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\operatorname{refused}(a) \neq \operatorname{answerable}\right], \\
\operatorname{CitationAligned}
  &= \mathbb{1}\!\left[\{\text{cited ids}\} \cap G \neq \emptyset\right].
\end{align}

Refusal is detected by matching a fixed list of abstention markers against the
lower-cased answer.

\paragraph{Aggregation.}
The report averages retrieval metrics and citation alignment over
\emph{answerable} cases only, because unanswerable cases have no relevant
documents; answer metrics are averaged over \emph{all} cases. Every aggregate
is the arithmetic mean rounded to four decimals, with the empty mean defined as
$0$:

\begin{equation}
\overline{m} = \operatorname{round}\!\left(\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}|}
  \sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}} m(c),\; 4\right),
\qquad \overline{m} = 0 \text{ when } \mathcal{C} = \emptyset .
\end{equation}

The report is a point estimate over a small, hand-authored case set. It carries
no confidence interval and no significance test, and the repository does not
claim any; its purpose is regression detection between revisions, for which a
deterministic point estimate is sufficient.

Implementation: \cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.evaluation.precision_at_k},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.evaluation.recall_at_k},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.evaluation.reciprocal_rank},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.evaluation.groundedness_score},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.evaluation.aggregate_report}.

\section{Latency statistics}
\label{sec:math-latency}

The benchmark harness times each pipeline phase over repeated calls after a
warm-up, and summarises with mean, median, minimum, maximum, and the 95th
percentile. The percentile uses the \emph{nearest-rank} method on the sorted
samples $y_{(1)} \leq \cdots \leq y_{(n)}$:

\begin{equation}
\operatorname{P}_{p} = y_{(\rho)},
\qquad
\rho = \min\!\left(\max\!\left(\left\lceil p\,n \right\rceil, 1\right), n\right),
\qquad p \in [0,1],
\end{equation}

with $\operatorname{P}_0 = y_{(1)}$ by definition. Nearest-rank always returns
an actually observed sample, which the source gives as the reason for
preferring it over interpolation for latency reporting: no value is fabricated
between measurements. Note the consequence for small $n$: with $n = 10$
repetitions, $\operatorname{P}_{0.95} = y_{(10)}$, the maximum. Percentile
estimates from small samples are coarse, and the harness exposes the sample
count alongside every statistic so this is visible in the report.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.benchmarking.percentile},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.benchmarking.summarize}.

\section{Synthetic data generation}
\label{sec:math-synthetic}

Synthetic datasets are drawn from a locally seeded
\texttt{random.Random} instance rather than from the global \texttt{random} or
NumPy state. The seed and the generation parameters fully determine the output:
the same configuration yields a byte-identical CSV, written with a fixed field
order, UTF-8 encoding, and \texttt{\textbackslash n} newlines. This is the
mechanism that makes generated datasets usable in regression tests, and it is
one of the six terms of the reproducibility equation documented in the
engineering part of the complete manual.

\medskip\noindent
Implementation: \cref{class:feedback_intelligence_agent.synthetic_data.SyntheticDataConfig},
\cref{func:feedback_intelligence_agent.synthetic_data.generate_feedback_rows}.

\section{References}

The BM25 formulation follows the Okapi weighting scheme of
Robertson and Sp\"arck~Jones~\cite{robertson1976relevance,robertson2009prf};
the signed feature-hashing construction follows
Weinberger et al.~\cite{weinberger2009hashing}; the retrieval metrics are
standard information-retrieval measures as presented by
Manning et al.~\cite{manning2008ir}. These references are supplied to identify
the methods implemented; the repository itself cites no literature.
